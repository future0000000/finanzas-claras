What Is Inflation and How It Affects Your Savings

How inflation erodes the purchasing power of your money over time, and strategies that exist to protect your savings.

Inflation is the general, sustained rise in the price of goods and services in an economy, which reduces the purchasing power of each unit of currency over time.

How it affects your savings

If your money sits without earning any return and annual inflation is, say, 5%, in real terms you're losing purchasing power every year, even though the number in your account doesn't go down.

Real interest vs nominal interest

Nominal interest is the rate the bank advertises; real interest is that rate minus inflation for the period. If your savings account pays 3% annually and inflation is 5%, your real interest is negative — meaning you lose purchasing power even while earning interest.

Strategies to protect against inflation

Seeking instruments whose returns consistently outpace historical inflation, diversifying across different asset types, and avoiding holding large sums of cash without returns for long periods are commonly used strategies.

Inflation doesn't affect everyone equally

People with fixed incomes that don't adjust for inflation are hit hardest, while those holding assets that appreciate over time (like certain real estate or investments) may be less affected.

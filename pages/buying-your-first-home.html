How to Buy Your First Home

A guide to the essential financial steps before buying your first home, from the down payment to the mortgage.

Buying a first home is often the biggest financial decision many people make in their lives, so it's worth preparing well in advance.

Step 1: Clean up your credit history

Banks review your payment history before approving a mortgage. Arriving with a strong credit history improves both your approval odds and the interest rate offered.

Step 2: Save for the down payment

Most mortgages require an upfront down payment, typically a percentage of the total property value. A larger down payment generally reduces the financed amount and, with it, the total interest cost.

Step 3: Calculate your real payment capacity

A common benchmark is that your monthly mortgage payment shouldn't exceed 25-30% of your net monthly income, leaving room for other expenses, savings, and emergencies.

Step 4: Compare offers from different banks

Rates and terms vary between institutions. Getting quotes from several banks before deciding can make a significant difference in the total cost of the loan over the years.

Step 5: Factor in additional purchase costs

Beyond the property price, there are notary, closing, appraisal, and transfer tax costs that should be budgeted separately from the down payment.

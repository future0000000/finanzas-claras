Credit Card vs Debit Card

Understand the real differences between credit and debit cards, their advantages, risks, and when to use each one.

Although they look almost identical, both cards work in completely different ways, and understanding the difference avoids costly mistakes.

Debit card

It's directly connected to your bank account. When you pay with it, the money leaves your available funds immediately. You can't spend more than you have (except with authorized overdrafts), which makes it safer for controlling spending.

Credit card

The bank lends you the money at the time of purchase, and you pay it back later, usually by the end of the month. If you don't pay the full balance, the remaining amount accrues interest, which tends to be high compared to other types of loans.

Advantages of a well-used credit card

It helps build credit history, often offers rewards or cashback programs, and provides extra protection on purchases and disputes with merchants.

Main risks

The most common risk is paying only the minimum each month, which lets the balance grow through compound interest, as mentioned earlier. Treating it like your own money without a repayment plan is the number one cause of personal overindebtedness.

General recommendation

Using your credit card only for expenses you've already budgeted for, and paying it off in full every month, combines the best of both worlds: credit history and zero interest.

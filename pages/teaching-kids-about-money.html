How to Teach Your Kids About Money

Age-by-age practical ideas for teaching financial education to children starting from a young age.

Childhood financial education lays the foundation for money habits that often carry through into adult life, and it can be introduced simply from an early age.

Young kids (ages 5-8): the concept of choosing

At this age, the goal is for them to understand that money is limited, and that spending it on one thing means not being able to spend it on something else. A visible savings jar for a small goal helps make the concept tangible.

Kids ages 9-12: allowance with responsibilities

Giving a fixed allowance tied to specific household chores introduces the connection between effort and income. Splitting the allowance into simple categories (spend, save, share) reinforces early planning habits.

Teens (ages 13-17): goals and first accounts

At this age, it's worth involving them in bigger savings goals (a device, a trip) and, where possible, opening a supervised teen bank account so they get familiar with real digital banking.

Young adults: credit and real budgeting

Explaining how credit works, compound interest, and helping them build their first real budget before they face big financial decisions on their own significantly reduces costly mistakes in early adulthood.

Example matters more than the speech

Kids constantly observe the financial habits of the adults around them; modeling good habits visibly tends to have more impact than just talking about the topic.

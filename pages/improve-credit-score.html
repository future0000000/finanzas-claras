How to Improve Your Credit Score

Concrete steps to improve your credit history and score, plus common mistakes that damage it without you realizing.

Your credit history is the record of how you've handled debt and payments in the past, and lenders use it to decide whether to lend you money and at what rate.

Factors that most affect your score

Payment history tends to carry the most weight, followed by your credit utilization ratio (ideally below 30%), the age of your accounts, the mix of credit types, and recent credit inquiries.

Strategy 1: always pay on time

Setting up automatic minimum payments, even if you plan to pay more, prevents accidental late payments that can hurt your history for years.

Strategy 2: keep your credit utilization low

Constantly using 90% of your card limit, even if you pay on time, can lower your score. Keeping it under 30% of your available limit improves how lenders perceive your risk.

Strategy 3: don't close old cards

Closing a card with good history reduces your average credit age and total available limit, which can negatively affect your score even if you no longer actively use that card.

Strategy 4: check your credit report regularly

Errors on credit reports are more common than you'd think. Reviewing your history at least once a year lets you catch and correct inaccurate information.

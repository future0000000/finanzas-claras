How to Plan for Retirement

Why starting to plan for retirement while young makes a huge difference, and how to calculate how much you'll need.

Retirement planning isn't just a topic for older people; the younger you start, the less money you need to contribute each month thanks to the effect of compound interest over decades.

Why starting early makes such a big difference

Because of cumulative growth, someone who starts saving at 25 may need to contribute a fraction of what someone starting at 45 would need to reach a similar goal, simply due to the extra years of growth.

How to estimate how much you'll need

A common reference point is calculating your expected annual expenses in retirement and multiplying by 25, based on the idea of withdrawing roughly 4% annually from the fund without depleting it too quickly. It's a general rule, not a guarantee.

Combine different income sources

Relying on a single source for retirement (for example, only public pension) tends to be risky. Combining personal savings, investments, and available pension systems in your country provides greater stability.

Review and adjust the plan periodically

Changes in income, family, or life goals require adjusting your retirement plan every few years, rather than setting it once and forgetting about it completely.

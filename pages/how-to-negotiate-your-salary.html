How to Negotiate Your Salary

Practical, data-based strategies to negotiate your salary in a new job offer or in your current role.

Negotiating your salary is one of the financial skills with the highest cumulative impact over a career, since every increase compounds on top of the next.

Research the market salary range

Before negotiating, it's important to know the market range for the role, industry, and region, using sources like industry salary surveys or specialized platforms.

Let the employer mention a number first

In many negotiations, whoever mentions a number first tends to anchor the conversation around that point. When possible, it's useful to let the employer propose a range first.

Base your ask on value, not need

Framing your request around the value you bring (results, experience, specific skills) tends to be more effective than framing it around personal needs, which aren't the employer's responsibility.

Consider the full package, not just base salary

Benefits, bonuses, vacation days, work flexibility, and growth opportunities are all part of an offer's total value, and sometimes there's more room to negotiate there than on base salary.

Practice the conversation in advance

Rehearsing the negotiation out loud, even with another person, helps reduce nervousness and respond more clearly to possible objections from the employer.

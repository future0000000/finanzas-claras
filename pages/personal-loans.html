Personal Loans: What to Consider

Key factors to evaluate before applying for a personal loan so you don't end up overleveraged.

A personal loan can be a useful tool or a source of financial trouble, depending on how and why it's used.

Evaluate the real reason for the loan

Using debt to finance something that generates future value (education, urgent repair of a productive asset) is very different from using it for consumer spending that generates no return, like vacations or non-essential purchases.

Compare the APR, not just the monthly payment

Two loans with the same monthly payment can have very different total costs depending on the term and effective rate. Always comparing the total annual rate, including fees, avoids unpleasant surprises.

Calculate whether the payment is sustainable

A common benchmark is that your total monthly debt payments (including the new loan) shouldn't exceed 30-40% of your net monthly income, leaving room for unexpected expenses.

Check prepayment penalties

Some loans charge a fee if you decide to pay them off before the agreed term. If you plan to pay early, verify this condition before signing.

Alternatives before taking on debt

Before applying for a loan, it's worth evaluating whether it's possible to save for the goal, negotiate terms directly with the provider, or use part of your emergency fund if applicable.

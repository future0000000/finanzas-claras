How to Make a Monthly Budget (Practical Guide)

Learn how to build a realistic monthly budget using the 50/30/20 method and take control of your spending starting today.

A monthly budget is simply a written plan of how much money comes in and how much goes out each month. It isn't about restricting yourself, but about deciding in advance where your money goes, instead of wondering at the end of the month where it disappeared to.

Step 1: Calculate your real net income

Add up all the money you actually receive each month after taxes: salary, side income, freelance work, etc. Always work with the net figure, never the gross salary, since that's the money actually available to spend.

Step 2: Use the 50/30/20 rule as a starting point

This widely used guideline suggests 50% of income for basic needs (rent, food, transportation, utilities), 30% for personal wants (entertainment, outings, non-essential purchases), and 20% for savings and debt repayment. It isn't a rigid law, but it gives a clear reference frame to get started.

Step 3: Track every expense for 30 days

Most people underestimate how much they spend on small categories like coffee, subscriptions, or impulse buys. Writing down every expense, even on your phone, reveals patterns that normally go unnoticed.

Step 4: Adjust categories to fit your reality

If you live in an expensive city, your housing percentage will likely exceed the recommended 30%; what matters is that income minus fixed expenses leaves real room for savings, even if it's small at the start.

Step 5: Review the budget every month

A budget isn't static. Reviewing it monthly lets you correct deviations before they turn into debt, and celebrate the months you actually hit your goals.

Stocks vs Bonds

Understand the fundamental difference between investing in stocks and bonds, their risks, and expected returns.

Stocks and bonds are the two most common types of investment instruments, but they represent completely different things.

What a stock is

Buying a stock means buying a small piece of ownership in a company. If the company grows and creates value, the stock price can rise; if the company loses value, the price can fall, even to zero in extreme cases.

What a bond is

A bond is essentially a loan you make to a company or government. In exchange, they commit to returning the principal on a future date and paying you periodic interest during the bond's term.

Difference in risk and return

Stocks tend to offer higher potential returns over the long term, but with greater short-term volatility. Bonds, especially government bonds from stable countries, usually offer more modest but more predictable returns.

Priority order in case of bankruptcy

If a company goes bankrupt, bondholders get paid before shareholders, which in practice makes corporate bonds generally less risky than that same company's stock.

How they combine in a portfolio

Many investment strategies combine both asset types in different proportions based on the investor's age and risk tolerance, aiming to balance growth and stability.

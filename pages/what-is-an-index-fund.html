What Is an Index Fund

A simple explanation of what index funds are, how they work, and why they're popular among beginner investors.

An index fund is an investment vehicle designed to replicate the performance of a specific stock market index, rather than trying to beat it through active stock selection.

How it works in practice

If an index fund tracks an index made up of 500 large companies, the fund buys shares of those same 500 companies proportionally, aiming for its performance to closely match that of the full index.

Main advantage: instant diversification

Buying a single share of an index fund gives you exposure to hundreds of different companies at once, reducing the risk that poor performance from a single company will seriously hurt your investment.

Generally lower costs

Since they don't require a team of analysts actively picking what to buy and sell, index funds tend to have significantly lower management fees than actively managed funds.

Historical performance vs active funds

Numerous long-term studies have shown that most actively managed funds fail to consistently outperform their benchmark index after fees are deducted, which has driven the popularity of index funds among long-term investors.

Considerations before investing

Although simpler than other alternatives, index funds are still subject to the general volatility of the market they track, so their value can drop during economic downturns.

Emergency Fund: How Much to Save

How much money you need in your emergency fund, how to build it from scratch, and which accounts to keep it in.

An emergency fund is money set aside exclusively for genuine unexpected events: job loss, medical bills, urgent repairs. It's not for vacations or planned purchases.

How much is recommended?

The general benchmark is 3 to 6 months of essential expenses (not your full income). If your income is unstable (freelance, commissions), aim closer to 6-9 months; if you have stable, fixed income, 3 months is usually enough.

How to build it from zero

Start with a small, achievable goal, like covering one month of basic expenses, before thinking about the full 6 months. Automating a fixed percentage every payday, even a modest one, is more effective than trying to save "whatever is left over."

Where to keep your emergency fund

It should be somewhere quickly accessible but separate from your everyday spending account, like a high-yield savings account. It shouldn't be invested in high-risk or low-liquidity instruments, since the goal isn't returns, it's immediate availability.

When to use it (and when not to)

Only use it for real, unexpected emergencies. If you use it for something planned, like a vacation, it stops serving its purpose and leaves you exposed right when a real emergency hits.

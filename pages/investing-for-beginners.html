How to Start Investing as a Beginner

Essential concepts to start investing from scratch, with no prior knowledge, in a responsible way.

Investing doesn't require being an economics expert, but it does require understanding a few basic concepts before putting money on the line.

First: define your time horizon

Money you'll need in less than 2 years shouldn't be invested in high-risk instruments; for short-term goals, conservative instruments are more appropriate. Investing in higher-risk markets is normally associated with horizons of 5 years or more.

Second: understand the risk-return relationship

Generally, higher potential return means higher risk of loss. There's no investment with guaranteed high returns and zero risk; any offer that promises that should be treated with extreme caution.

Common options for beginners

Index funds that track a broad stock market index are frequently recommended for beginners because of their automatic diversification and lower management costs compared to actively managed funds.

The importance of diversifying

Putting all your money into a single stock or asset greatly increases risk. Spreading your investment across different sectors and asset types reduces the impact if one of them performs poorly.

Before you invest

It's generally recommended to first have an emergency fund in place and high-interest debt under control, since the cost of that debt usually outweighs the average return of any investment.

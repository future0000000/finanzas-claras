What Is Compound Interest

A clear explanation of compound interest, how it's calculated, and why it's the most powerful tool in long-term saving.

Compound interest is interest calculated not only on the money you originally deposited, but also on the interest you've already earned before. Put simply, it's "interest on interest."

A simple example

If you invest 1,000 at a 10% annual rate, by the end of year one you'll have 1,100. In year two, the 10% is no longer calculated on 1,000 but on 1,100, so you'll earn 110 instead of 100. That difference, small at first, becomes enormous over time.

Why time matters more than the amount

Because of this cumulative effect, investing small amounts starting at age 25 usually generates more wealth long-term than investing large amounts starting at 40, simply because the money has more years to grow on itself.

The dark side: debt compounds too

The same principle works against you with high-interest credit card debt: unpaid interest gets added to the principal and generates more interest the following month, so a small unpaid debt can grow very fast.

How to use it in your favor

Contributing consistently, even in small amounts, and reinvesting any returns generated (instead of withdrawing them) is the simplest way to make compound interest work for you.
